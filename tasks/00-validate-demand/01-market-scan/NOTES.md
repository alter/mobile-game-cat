# Market scan — "photograph your own cat"

Scope per task.txt: 10 comparable titles (tile/pile-match or pet-care casual
games) with revenue estimates and sourcing, plus a named check for whether
the exact mechanic — the player photographs her own pet and the game uses
that photo as the in-game character — already exists.

Pitch and audience checked against `cat-shelter-mvp.md` §1–2: a pile-clearing
puzzle where, on entry, the player photographs her own real cat and the
in-game kitten gets its coloring; audience is women 30–55, 10–20 minute
sessions, driven by order-from-disorder, caring for something weaker that's
getting better, completeness, and coziness.

Research done: 2026-08-27, via web search (Jina search/read tools, which
query Google) and direct reads of Sensor Tower's free public overview pages.
No App Store/Google Play accounts were used, nothing paywalled was accessed.

## 2. Named check: does "photograph your own pet" already exist as a game mechanic?

**Question:** is there a tile/pile-match or pet-care casual game, on the App
Store or Google Play, where the player photographs her own real pet at
onboarding (or any point) and the game uses that photo to generate or skin
the in-game character?

**Where searched:** live web search (via Jina's search_web /
search_web_deep, which query Google), aimed at both general web coverage and
App Store / Google Play listing text. No direct in-store search-bar query
was run inside the App Store or Google Play apps themselves — see the VERIFY
gap below.

**Searches run** (all 2026-08-27):
- "app photograph your own cat game upload photo AI cat game App Store"
- "\"take a photo of your cat\" mobile game App Store"
- "cat photo scan game generate cat character app"
- "CatchCat app camera collectible cats App Store Google Play"
- "site:apps.apple.com CatchCat"
- "Disco Cats augmented reality dance game your cat face App Store"
- "app store \"your own cat\" pet care game photo becomes character"
- "google play cat pile match game photograph your cat kitten"
- "Secret Cat Forest game upload your own cat photo App Store Google Play"
- "\"Secret Cat Forest\" game description real cat photos characters"

**What came back — three near-misses, no exact match:**

1. **Secret Cat Forest** (IDEASAM, iOS + Android, live) is built entirely
   from photographs of real cats used as in-game characters — but they are
   cats sourced by the developer through community events on the studio's
   Korean Naver Cafe, not a player-facing "photograph your own pet" feature.
   A 2023 Facebook thread in the game's own fan group
   (facebook.com/groups/secretcatforest/posts/1305537703686770) has a player
   asking whether she can submit photos of her friend's cat, who had just
   died, to be added as a character; a group moderator answers that the
   studio "did try to do it worldwide a long time ago but ran into potential
   issues with trademarking" and now only adds cats through occasional
   Korean-market social events. This is the closest real precedent to the
   pitch's emotional target — and the record of *why it was pulled back* is
   itself a finding worth weighing before committing to the same mechanic.
2. **Disco Cats — Augmented Reality Dance Game** (not a pile-match or
   pet-care title; an AR toy) let a player "take a photo of your cat from
   within the app, line up their face" to map her own cat's face onto a
   dancing AR avatar. Per a third-party archive note
   (disco-cats-augmented-reality-dance-game-free-ios.soft112.com), it was
   removed from the App Store on 2026-05-27 — a dead precedent, not a live
   competitor, and never in the pile-match/pet-care genre to begin with.
3. **CatchCat / "CatchCat – Catemon" / "Kitty Go: CatchCat" / "catch (cat
   tracker)"** — a cluster of near-identical, very recently launched apps
   (App Store listings dated Jul–Aug 2026) that use the phone camera to
   photograph and AI-generate collectible cards from cats the player
   encounters outdoors — explicitly a Pokémon-Go-style hunt for *any* cat on
   the street, not the player's own single companion cat, and the resulting
   card is a keepsake/collectible rather than a cared-for character in a
   puzzle or sim loop.

**Finding: no exact match.** No tile/pile-match or pet-care casual game
currently on the App Store or Google Play was found where photographing the
player's own pet drives the in-game character's look as a core, built-in
mechanic. This is a negative result, not proof of absence — see the VERIFY
note in task.txt: a second person should re-run this as direct searches
typed into the App Store and Google Play search bars themselves (e.g. "photo
your cat game," "your cat game," "cat photo game," "pet photo game"), since
this scan used web-indexed search rather than the stores' own search/ASO
indexes, which can surface different results (as the CatchCat cluster,
apparently very new, illustrates — store search may turn up even more recent
entrants than a web crawl has indexed yet).

## 3. What this scan does NOT establish

- **Not a revenue forecast.** Comparable titles' revenue says nothing about
  what this game would earn; it only bounds "is there money in adjacent
  genres" (yes, unevenly — from $3M/month for the pile-match category leader
  down to five figures for niche cat-collectors).
- **Not global totals.** Every Sensor Tower figure here is a single
  storefront/country snapshot (mostly US iOS, two rows worldwide-iOS-only);
  real global, cross-platform revenue for these titles is higher than what's
  quoted, by an unknown multiple this scan did not attempt to estimate.
- **Not a precision estimate.** Sensor Tower's own methodology page
  (appmagic.rocks/articles/how-metrics-work) states its own reported
  adjustments run up to 10% for downloads and up to 2% for IAP revenue at
  the aggregate/genre level; individual small-app numbers, especially the
  "<5k downloads" / "<$5K revenue" rows here, carry wider and unstated error
  bars that this scan did not quantify — treat them as order-of-magnitude,
  not exact.
- **Not proof the mechanic has never existed.** The negative result in §2
  covers what surfaced in a web search done on one day; it is not an
  exhaustive audit of both stores' full catalogs, and per VERIFY item 2 in
  task.txt, needs a second person's direct in-store search to confirm.
- **Not a go/no-go call.** That decision belongs to
  `tasks/00-validate-demand/09-go-no-go`, per task.txt's stated scope
  boundary.

## 2026-08-27 — follow-up: is the Secret Cat Forest precedent solid, and what actually stands in the way

The §2 finding above reported a fan-group thread as the closest precedent to
this project's "it's her cat, from her photo" premise, and as one studio's
account of pulling that exact feature back over trademark concerns. That
claim was checked against a primary source, and the surrounding legal/policy
ground was mapped. Full detail, with every quote sourced and dated, is in
`legal-risk.md` in this same directory. Summary:

- **The precedent does not hold up.** It traces to one sentence from a fan
  group *moderator* — not IDEASAM staff — with no date, no link, and no
  corroboration found anywhere, including IDEASAM's own site and the game's
  community wiki. Treat it as an unverified anecdote, not evidence against
  the premise.
- **Apple's and Google's UGC moderation rules (1.2 / the UGC policy) almost
  certainly don't apply** to the photo capture feature as designed: both
  platforms define UGC as content visible to other users, and per D8 the
  cat photo is never shown to anyone but the player who took it.
- **A real correction is needed**, and it isn't the trademark story:
  `cat-shelter-tech.md` §3 quotes Anthropic as saying photo uploads are
  "ephemeral and not stored beyond the duration of the API request." That
  exact sentence could not be found anywhere on Anthropic's current sites.
  What Anthropic's privacy center currently states (retrieved 2026-08-27) is
  that standard API accounts retain inputs/outputs for **up to 30 days**;
  true zero-retention is an opt-in enterprise arrangement reached by
  contacting Anthropic sales, not the default. Anthropic does not use
  retained data for training without express permission, so that risk holds
  up — but "not stored" does not, and the same "not stored server-side"
  phrasing already sits in `tasks/60-shell-build/14-testflight/task.txt` and
  is implied in `tasks/90-android/10-permission-audit/task.txt`; both need
  the corrected wording before their privacy declarations are filled in.
- Real, unglamorous constraints that do apply regardless: privacy-policy
  links, camera/photo purpose strings, and the App Store/Play Store privacy
  disclosures naming Anthropic as a data processor — see `legal-risk.md` §2
  for citations. Google Play's Photo and Video Permissions policy (in force
  since 2025-01-22) is a real constraint the project already designs around
  correctly, per the picker-plugin choice named in
  `tasks/90-android/10-permission-audit/task.txt`.

See `legal-risk.md` for the full sourcing, every search run, and the plain
list of real constraints vs. myths vs. open decisions.
