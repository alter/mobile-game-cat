# Why this runs before the ad spend, not after

The original plan asked for three props at M0 (0.3) and deferred the cat
entirely to task 4.4 in 40-art - a task whose own note calls it "the single
largest project risk", where an agent "may fail outright" and the fallback is
hiring an artist. Under that plan, $300 and the whole build get committed on
the strength of a creative before anyone finds out whether the art it
promised can actually be made.

The creatives that sell the demand test (0.4.1 "room before/after" and 0.4.2
"photograph your cat -> it appears in game") cannot be filmed without a cat
that visibly improves. So this task exists: a small slice of 4.4 pulled
forward, one cat in two states. If it fails, the project's worst news is
learned for zero dollars instead of $300 and three weeks.

Second, sharper reason: a creative promising art the game cannot deliver
makes the M0 cost-per-install meaningless as a predictor - it would measure
demand for a game that does not exist and cannot be built. Whatever appears
in the creatives must come from the same pipeline as the real game.

Two attempts at the six-silhouette cat, then hire an artist - see
art-brief.md section 7. That rule belongs to 40-art/4.4, not to this pilot,
but the same pipeline is being validated here first.
