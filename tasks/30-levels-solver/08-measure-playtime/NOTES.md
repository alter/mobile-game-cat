# Why this check exists, and why its result was a problem

Metric 3 (day-1 return) decides the project. If twelve levels take 40 minutes
and the target player sits down for 10-20 minutes, a keen player finishes the
entire game before day 1 arrives - and "did not return" then means "had
nothing left to play", not "did not want to come back". The metric would
measure content volume while appearing to measure desire. Get this number
before M8, when it is cheap to learn and expensive to discover afterwards.

## The result

The owner played all twelve levels on the refactored 36/48/60 pile-size curve:
**576 taps, 14 minutes brisk, 24 minutes unhurried.** That is the entire
content of the MVP, consumed in one sitting. Verdict on feel: mildly
enjoyable, doubtful it lasts.

This is exactly the failure the check existed to catch. It directly triggered
two later decisions: the room/pile restructuring to 37 levels (05,
DECISIONS.md D2) and hidden kinds to make the pile itself more absorbing (09,
DECISIONS.md D3). At 37 levels across the same pacing curve the estimated
total is roughly 44-74 minutes by tap count - a floor, since hidden kinds and
complications both slow play further.
